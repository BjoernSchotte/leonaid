## Design Context

### Users

In the PoC, LeonAid is used mainly by volunteer Lions members. Charity admins
organize a time-limited charity action, mostly on a desktop and often in short
time windows. Acquirers often work on the move, have varying technical
confidence and must record companies, contacts, follow-ups, commitments and
orders quickly and reliably. Public customers later use short action pages and
forms without signing in.

### Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm and action-oriented. The interface should convey the
reliability of a non-profit organization without looking like a traditional
club portal. It conveys overview and confidence, stays human and avoids
technical or bureaucratic language.

### Aesthetic Direction

A modern, precise work interface with a clear information hierarchy, generous
white space and deliberate density where recurring workflows benefit from it.
The official Lions International brand colors are the basis; LeonAid uses them
semantically and with restraint, not as decoration. Light, dark and system
mode are equal. Hugeicons and the shared shadcn/ui-based component library
give a consistent but not generic look. Anti-references are cluttered club
portals, generic 2024/2025 SaaS dashboards, decorative card grids,
glassmorphism and visual effects without business meaning.

### Design Principles

1. A page has one recognizable main task and exactly one dominant action.
2. Wording follows the daily work of Lions members; technical terms and
   internal IDs stay invisible.
3. Mobile interfaces are rearranged for their usage context, not just scaled
   down; core actions stay reachable and at least 44 px in size.
4. Status, consequences and next steps are always clear, even without color,
   hover state or remembering previous pages.
5. Reusable tokens and components ensure consistency, while typography, rhythm
   and deliberate brand accents make LeonAid distinctive.
6. Accessibility, keyboard operation, reduced motion and robust error states
   are part of the base quality, not later polish.
